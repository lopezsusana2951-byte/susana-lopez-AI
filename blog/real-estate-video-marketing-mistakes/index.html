Real Estate Video Marketing Tips: 7 Mistakes Agents Make (and How to Fix Them)

By Susana Lopez  ·  May 27, 2026  ·  Marketing Tips

Most agents know that real estate video marketing tips point in one direction: video converts better than photos, full stop. But knowing video matters and executing it correctly are two different things. After working with luxury agents across multiple markets, the same seven mistakes appear over and over — each one quietly costing inquiries, engagement, and listings.

Here is what those mistakes look like, and exactly how to fix them.

Mistake 1: Treating Video as Optional

The most expensive mistake on this list

Listings with professional video generate 403% more inquiries than listings with photos alone. Yet the majority of agents still treat video as a premium add-on reserved for ultra-luxury listings, rather than a baseline requirement for every property in their portfolio.

The fix is a mindset shift: video is not a marketing upgrade. It is the listing. Photos are the supporting documentation. Buyers — especially international buyers who cannot visit in person — make shortlist decisions based on video. If your listing does not have one, it does not exist for that buyer segment.

AI video production makes this shift financially viable. A monthly content package starting at $599/month covers multiple listings with cinematic video, not just one.

Mistake 2: Shooting Vertical for YouTube and Horizontal for Instagram

Format confusion kills reach

Instagram is a vertical platform. YouTube is horizontal. Posting a 16:9 YouTube tour to Instagram Reels — or a 9:16 vertical Reel to YouTube — signals immediately that the content was not made for the platform the viewer is on. Algorithms punish format mismatches with reduced distribution.

The fix: plan both cuts from the start. AI video production workflows make this simple — the same property assets are rendered in both orientations without a second shoot. For a deeper breakdown of which format performs where, see our guide to short-form vs. long-form real estate video.

Every property deserves both a vertical Reel and a horizontal tour — same assets, different cuts

Mistake 3: No Hook in the First Three Seconds

You lose 60% of viewers before the fourth second

On Instagram and TikTok, viewers decide whether to keep watching within the first three seconds. Most real estate videos open with a logo animation, a slow pan of the exterior, or a title card. None of these stop the scroll.

The fix: open with the most dramatic frame in the property — the view, the pool at golden hour, the double-height living room ceiling. Lead with impact. The address, price, and agent branding can come after you have earned the viewer's attention. AI video production tools allow precise control over the opening frame, making it possible to A/B test different hooks for the same listing.

Mistake 4: English-Only Content in a Multilingual Market

You are invisible to half your buyer pool

The luxury real estate market is global. Latin American buyers — from Mexico, Colombia, Brazil, Venezuela, and Argentina — represent one of the largest and fastest-growing international buyer segments. Most of them prefer to consume content in Spanish, especially when making a decision of this magnitude.

Producing content exclusively in English means those buyers scroll past your listings without engaging. The fix is bilingual content from day one — not translated captions added as an afterthought, but content crafted natively in both languages. Every Susana Lopez package includes bilingual English and Spanish content as a baseline. Mistake 5: No Call to Action

Views without direction produce zero inquiries

A listing video that ends with a fade to black and a logo is not a marketing asset — it is a brochure without a phone number. Buyers who watch your video and want to know more need an immediate, frictionless path to contact you.

The fix: every video ends with a visible CTA. On Instagram, this means a caption with your WhatsApp number or booking link and a final frame with contact information. On YouTube, an end screen with a subscribe button and a link to your contact page. The goal is zero friction between "I want this property" and "I am talking to the agent." Every video needs a visible path from interest to inquiry — no exceptions

Mistake 6: Inconsistent Posting Schedule

Algorithms reward consistency, not effort

Posting three videos in one week and then going silent for a month is worse than posting once a week consistently. Instagram and YouTube both prioritize accounts that publish on a predictable schedule. An inconsistent posting pattern tells the algorithm — and your audience — that you are not reliable.

The fix: a monthly content calendar with pre-produced assets ready to deploy. A monthly content package solves this structurally — it produces all the content for the month upfront, so you or your team can schedule posts in advance and maintain consistency without scrambling. Mistake 7: Using Phone-Shot Video for Luxury Listings

The listing reflects the agent's standard

In luxury real estate, every piece of content is a representation of the agent's brand. A phone-shot walkthrough video — shaky camera, harsh natural light, no music, no editing — communicates that the agent does not take presentation seriously. The property deserves better. So does the agent.

This does not mean spending $10,000 on a crew for every listing. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the biggest mistake agents make with real estate video marketing?
Treating video as optional is the most costly mistake. Listings with professional video generate 403% more inquiries than photo-only listings. Most agents still treat video as a premium add-on when it is the primary decision-making tool for today's buyers — especially international ones who cannot visit in person.
How long should a real estate marketing video be?
For Instagram Reels: 30–60 seconds. For YouTube property tours: 3–8 minutes. For agent branding content: 60–90 seconds. The mistake most agents make is creating one generic video at the wrong length for every platform, instead of editing format-specific cuts from the same source material.
Why does my real estate video get views but no inquiries?
Views without inquiries usually means the video lacks a clear call to action, the contact information is not visible, or the content attracts curiosity instead of buyer intent. Videos need to qualify the viewer — showing price range, location context, and lifestyle fit — so only motivated buyers reach out.
Should real estate video be in English and Spanish?
Yes, for any agent targeting international buyers or markets with significant Spanish-speaking populations. Bilingual video content reaches buyers who will scroll past English-only listings without engaging. Every Susana Lopez package includes bilingual English and Spanish content at no extra charge.
How often should agents post real estate video content?
Consistency matters more than volume. One high-quality video per week outperforms five mediocre ones. The agents who win on social are posting 3–4 times per week across formats — a mix of property tours, lifestyle content, and market insight — on a predictable schedule that builds audience expectations.
